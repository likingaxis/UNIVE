# BRIEF.md — Donne, gaming e inclusione

## 1. Project Overview

**Project title:** Donne, gaming e inclusione

**Format:** 9-slide academic presentation, 16:9

**Language:** Italian

**Context:** University presentation for Gender & Inclusion, Computer Science, Università di Roma Tor Vergata.

**Primary goal:** Redesign the existing presentation from the ground up while preserving the strongest content, arguments, data, and headline ideas from the oral script and current deck.

The final deck should be:
- visually stronger;
- more editorial;
- more memorable;
- easier to present orally;
- less repetitive;
- less dashboard-like;
- more clearly connected to videogame culture.

The deck must remain academically credible and suitable for projection in a classroom.

## 3. Narrative Structure

The presentation follows this logic:

### 1. Introduce the topic
Gaming is not only entertainment. It is:
- software;
- a cultural medium;
- a social space.

### 2. Explain why this matters to computer scientists
Technical decisions can influence participation, safety, representation and access.

### 3. Establish the perceptual paradox
Women are already close to half of the gaming audience, but gaming is still culturally perceived as male.

### 4. Show the cost of visibility
When gender becomes identifiable, especially through voice chat, harassment can increase and some players respond by becoming less visible.

### 5. Show change in representation
Female characters evolve from passive / objectified roles toward more complex and autonomous subjects.

### 6. Move behind the screen
Near-parity among players is not reflected in the workforce, especially in technical roles.

### 7. Show how the gap grows with seniority and competition
Pay gap, career progression and esports illustrate structural barriers.

### 8. Move from diversity to inclusion
Inclusion is not merely representation. It requires design choices, safe communities and equitable opportunities.

### 9. Conclude
Women already belong to gaming. The unresolved question is whether gaming environments are designed so that everyone can participate under equal conditions.

---

## 4. Core Thesis

The central thesis of the deck is:

**The problem is not whether women belong to gaming. They already do. The issue is whether gaming environments, representations and professional structures allow equal participation.**

A secondary thesis is especially important for the academic context:

**Inclusion can be treated as a design requirement, not only as a social issue external to technology.**

## 14. Anti-Generic Requirements

These requirements are mandatory.

Do not default to:
- generic card grids;
- SaaS dashboard layouts;
- equal three-column systems;
- glassmorphism;
- repeated rounded rectangles;
- excessive icons;
- excessive neon;
- unnecessary gradients;
- generic stock imagery;
- generic AI-generated composition.

Before using a box, card, icon, image or decorative element, ask:
**Does this improve comprehension, hierarchy or narrative?**

If not, remove it.
