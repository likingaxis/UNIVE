# DESIGN.md — Donne, gaming e inclusione

## 1. Design Concept

**Working direction:** `Academic Gaming Editorial`

The deck combines:
- refined academic presentation design;
- premium editorial composition;
- late-1990s / early-2000s videogame magazine DNA;
- subtle contemporary gaming language.

The intended balance is approximately:
- **60–70% premium academic presentation**
- **30–40% videogame magazine editorial influence**

The final result should feel:
- adult;
- cultured;
- polished;
- visually distinctive;
- presentation-first;
- appropriate for a university setting;
- recognizably connected to videogame culture without becoming themed or childish.

It must **not** feel:
- corporate;
- SaaS/dashboard-like;
- Canva-template-like;
- excessively retro;
- nostalgic for nostalgia’s sake;
- cluttered;
- childish;
- “AI-generated” in a generic way.

---

## 2. Core Principle

Every slide should communicate **one dominant idea**.

The slide should work at three reading speeds:

1. **3 seconds** — the thesis is immediately understandable.
2. **10 seconds** — the main visual / data / contrast is clear.
3. **30 seconds** — supporting context can be read if needed.

Slides support the oral presentation. They must not reproduce the oral script.

Use a **medium amount of text**:
- enough context to allow discussion;
- enough detail to preserve academic credibility;
- never dense paragraphs.

### Keywords

- academic

## 4. Background and Surface

Use a **graphite / charcoal / warm dark gray** foundation rather than pure black.

Recommended visual range:

- `#17191D`
- `#202329`
- `#292C32`

These are directional, not mandatory exact values.

### Reason

The presentation must remain legible on an average university projector.

Avoid:
- OLED-black backgrounds;
- low-contrast dark-on-dark details;
- overly subtle gradients;
- thin gray text that may disappear when projected.

Use:
- gentle tonal separation;
- slightly elevated panels only when functionally necessary;
- restrained gradients;
- occasional editorial blocks / strips.

---

## 5. Color System

Use three principal accent colors with semantic meaning.

### Cyan
**Function:** technology, data, systems, information.

Use for:
- key percentages;
- technical concepts;
- neutral data emphasis;
- section accents.

### Violet
**Function:** identity, representation, culture, character design.

Use for:
- representation-related content;
- timeline moments;
- identity / cultural commentary.

### Coral / Warm Red
**Function:** toxicity, inequality, barriers, conflict.

Use for:
- harassment;
- pay gaps;
- exclusion;
- negative contrasts;
- high-tension slides.

### Neutrals
Use:
- off-white for primary text;
- pale gray for secondary text;
- muted gray for metadata;
- graphite / charcoal for backgrounds.

Avoid:
- rainbow dashboard palettes;
- assigning a new color to every category;
- saturated accents everywhere.

Color should carry meaning, not decoration.

---

## 6. Typography

Use a controlled editorial mix.

### A. Display Sans
Primary use:
- slide titles;
- hero statements;
- large data labels.

Character:
- strong;
- contemporary;
- slightly condensed or semi-condensed if appropriate;
- premium;
- highly legible.

### B. Readable Sans
Primary use:
- body copy;
- captions;
- metadata;
- data labels;
- annotations;
- source lines.

Character:
- neutral;
- clean;
- highly readable at projection distance.

### C. Serif Accent
Optional and sparse.

Maximum use:
- approximately **10–15% of the deck**.

Possible use:
- editorial quote;
- small kicker;
- selected emphasis;
- one or two high-impact moments.

Do not use serif and sans equally.

### Typography Rules

- Prefer short, strong headlines.
- Use sentence case unless a label requires uppercase.
- Avoid fully uppercase body text.
- Use uppercase selectively for metadata and small editorial labels.
- Avoid too many weights.
- Keep body text comfortably large for projection.
- Do not shrink text to solve layout problems.

---

## 7. Layout System

Use three broad layout families.

### 1. Hero / Statement
Use for:
- cover;
- manifesto slides;
- conclusion;
- selected high-impact moments.

Characteristics:
- one dominant visual or statement;
- strong negative space;
- minimal supporting content.

### 2. Analytical / Data
Use for:
- percentages;
- comparisons;
- gaps;
- structured evidence.

Characteristics:
- one hero statistic or relationship;
- secondary context;
- editorial data visualization;
- no generic KPI-card grids.

### 3. Editorial / Narrative
Use for:
- timelines;
- conceptual frameworks;
- progression;
- character / image-led storytelling.

Characteristics:
- layering;
- controlled asymmetry;
- labels;
- image-text relationships;
- magazine-inspired composition.

Maintain a coherent grid across all three families.

---

## 8. Grid and Composition

Prefer a disciplined underlying grid with controlled breaks.

### General rules

- Large outer margins.
- Consistent title zone.
- Consistent footer / metadata zone.
- Use 2-column or 3-column logic where useful.
- Allow selected elements to break the grid intentionally.
- Avoid centering everything.
- Favor asymmetry when it improves hierarchy.
- Do not fill empty space simply because it exists.

### Controlled grid-breaking

Allowed:
- character cut-out extending above a timeline;
- large statistic crossing a column boundary;
- image overlapping a divider;
- editorial label extending beyond a text block.

Not allowed:
- random floating elements;
- arbitrary diagonal placement;
- collage without visual hierarchy.

---

## 9. Image Treatment

Images must have a narrative purpose.

### Use images when they:
- represent a character discussed;
- contextualize a game or environment;
- strengthen a historical progression;
- make an abstract concept concrete.

### Do not use images when they:
- merely fill empty space;
- duplicate what text already says;
- are generic stock decoration.

### Character Art

Preferred treatment:
- clean cut-out;
- transparent background when possible;
- editorial placement;
- controlled scale;
- integrated with timeline or headline.

Avoid:
- fan-poster collage;
- too many characters per slide;
- inconsistent rendering styles when avoidable;
- excessive glow around cut-outs.

### Screenshots

Use:
- clean crops;
- cinematic crops where appropriate;
- frames only when they support the composition;
- subtle treatment consistent with the deck.

Avoid:
- screenshot walls;
- random tilted screenshots;
- noisy borders.

---

## 10. Late-90s / Early-2000s Magazine DNA

The deck may borrow selected visual behaviors from videogame magazines of that era.

### Allowed influences

- strong headline scale;
- bold editorial labels;
- cropped character art;
- timeline markers;
- small side annotations;
- callout boxes;
- asymmetrical image placement;
- occasional full-bleed or near-full-bleed image;
- section identifiers;
- typographic contrast;
- occasional “feature article” composition.

### Modernization rule

Every retro influence must be filtered through:
- cleaner spacing;
- fewer elements;
- stronger hierarchy;
- better alignment;
- more restraint;
- modern readability.

Use the **energy** of those magazines, not their clutter.

---

## 11. Gaming Language

Gaming references should be subtle.

### Allowed

- section numbers;
- metadata;
- small tags;
- progress markers;
- thin interface-like dividers;
- small UI-inspired indicators;
- restrained coordinate / system labels;
- editorial “module” labels.

### Avoid

- pixel fonts;
- scanlines;
- CRT effects;
- glitch transitions;
- neon overload;
- cyberpunk clichés;
- controller icons as generic decoration;
- HUD overlays that compete with content.

The deck should feel videogame-aware **after a few seconds**, not scream “gaming theme” immediately.

---

## 12. Data Visualization

Data should feel **designed**, not default.

### Principles

- Use real comparison logic.
- Make the relationship visually obvious.
- Keep source credibility.
- Prefer direct labeling over legends.
- Use annotation when helpful.
- Highlight the main insight, not every number equally.
- Use one dominant data story per slide.

### Suitable forms

- comparison bars;
- ratio / parity line;
- annotated percentage blocks;
- stepped progression;
- visual gap;
- editorial callout;
- timeline;
- simple directional flow.

### Avoid

- default spreadsheet charts;
- repeated KPI cards;
- decorative donut charts;
- charts with unnecessary axes;
- tiny legends;
- gradients used only for decoration.

---

## 13. Navigation System

Use a subtle, consistent navigation layer.

Recommended components:
- slide / section number;
- section name;
- speaker identity;
- optional restrained progress cue.

Example:

`03 / ESPERIENZA ONLINE`  
`02 — VALERIO BERNARDI`

Keep navigation visually secondary.

Avoid:
- large page numbers;
- oversized progress bars;
- duplicating multiple navigation systems.

---

## 14. Footer and Sources

Sources should be present but visually secondary.

### Throughout the deck
Use compact source references only where necessary.

### Final slide
Place the main source list discreetly.

Do not let the bibliography compete with the conclusion.

Use:
- small readable sans;
- muted neutral color;
- clean spacing;
- no large source card unless absolutely necessary.

---

## 15. Slide Rhythm

The deck should alternate visual intensity.

Suggested rhythm:

1. **Visual hero**
2. **Calm conceptual**
3. **Data**
4. **High-impact analytical**
5. **Hero visual / timeline**
6. **Data + contextual image**
7. **Analytical editorial**
8. **Manifesto**
9. **Quiet hero conclusion**

Avoid making all slides equally “busy”.

Use approximately **2–3 wow moments**.

The rest should be calmer and more disciplined.

## 16. Slide-Specific Intent

### Slide 1 — Donne, gaming e inclusione
Type: **Hero**

- Replace generic controller illustration.
- Use 2–3 carefully selected female videogame characters.
- Character art should be integrated editorially.
- Avoid fan-poster composition.
- Keep title dominant.
- Mood: premium cover / editorial opener.

### Slide 2 — Perché parlarne da informatici?
Type: **Editorial / conceptual**

Core framework:
- chi gioca;
- cosa vediamo;
- chi sviluppa.

Avoid three generic equal cards.

Communicate the idea that code and design shape participation in digital spaces.

### Slide 3 — La realtà è quasi paritaria. La percezione no.
Type: **Analytical**

- `48%` should be the primary data point.
- Supporting data: Europe, Italy, growth.
- Make the perceptual paradox visually evident.
- Avoid dashboard structure.
- Main takeaway: women already play; visibility remains unequal.

### Slide 4 — Quando basta una voce per diventare un bersaglio
Type: **High-impact analytical / narrative**

Visual logic:
`voice → gender becomes identifiable → harassment → self-protection / invisibility`

Use coral deliberately.

Support with the key percentages and the defensive behaviors:
- mute;
- neutral nickname;
- closed parties.

This is one of the higher-tension slides.

### Slide 5 — Da contorno a soggetto
Type: **Hero editorial timeline**

Use a horizontal timeline inspired by the supplied references.

Character progression:
- Peach;
- Lara;
- Aloy / Senua / Ellie;
- modular contemporary identity.

Use clean character cut-outs above their chronological points.

Allow characters and labels to break the grid.

Core takeaway:
**Da oggetto della narrazione a soggetto della narrazione.**

This should be one of the strongest visual slides in the deck.

### Slide 6 — Quasi metà gioca. Solo un quarto sviluppa.
Type: **Analytical + contextual image**

Primary progression:
`48% → 23–25% → ≈20%`

Use a real game-development-studio / workstation environment image.

The image should:
- contextualize development work;
- feel authentic;
- remain secondary to the argument.

Avoid generic startup-office imagery.

### Slide 7 — Il gap cresce salendo di livello
Type: **Editorial infographic**

Main topics:
- pay gap;
- seniority / compensation;
- esports.

Avoid three equal statistic cards.

Make the “gap increasing with level” the composition itself.

VCT Game Changers and ESL Impact should appear as responses / interventions, not as unrelated cards.

### Slide 8 — Non basta essere presenti: bisogna poter partecipare
Type: **Manifesto**

Core pillars:
- design inclusivo;
- community sicure;
- opportunità eque.

Core thesis:
**L’inclusione è un requisito di progettazione.**

Keep the slide visually clean and conceptually strong.

### Slide 9 — La presenza non è ancora piena inclusione
Type: **Quiet hero conclusion**

Dominant takeaway:
**Le donne nel gaming ci sono già.**

Secondary statement:
The real question is whether gaming environments are designed so that everyone can participate under equal conditions.

Keep sources visually secondary.

End with clarity, not information density.

## 17. Anti-AI-Slop Rules

These rules are mandatory.

### Never default to:
- three identical cards;
- four identical KPI cards;
- repeated rounded rectangles;
- generic glassmorphism;
- default icon + title + paragraph modules;
- excessive shadows;
- glowing borders around everything;
- random gradients;
- symmetric dashboard layouts on every slide;
- unnecessary pills and badges;
- “AI startup” aesthetics.

### Avoid generic visual habits
Do not:
- put every statistic inside a card;
- use icons to compensate for weak composition;
- use decorative lines without function;
- repeat the same layout across adjacent slides;
- create artificial hierarchy with too many colors;
- use small text to fit too much content.

### Prefer
- editorial composition;
- strong typography;
- meaningful negative space;
- image-led storytelling;
- direct data relationships;
- asymmetry;
- intentional overlap;
- clear thesis-first layouts.

Every element must justify its presence.

---

## 18. Projection and Accessibility

The deck will be shown in a university classroom.

Therefore:

- prioritize contrast;
- avoid tiny type;
- avoid thin low-contrast lines;
- avoid dark details hidden inside dark backgrounds;
- do not rely on subtle color distinctions alone;
- keep body text readable from distance;
- ensure hero statistics are immediately visible;
- do not make source text illegibly small.

The design should survive imperfect projector contrast.

---

## 19. Consistency Rules

Maintain consistency in:

- title typography;
- body typography;
- navigation placement;
- margins;
- spacing scale;
- accent color semantics;
- image treatment;
- source treatment;
- line weight;
- metadata styling.

Variation should come from composition, not from changing the visual identity.

---

## 20. Final Quality Bar

Before considering a slide finished, check:

- Is the core message obvious?
- Is there one dominant focal point?
- Does the slide support the speaker rather than replace them?
- Is the composition editorial rather than dashboard-like?
- Does the gaming influence feel mature and restrained?
- Is the late-90s / early-2000s magazine influence visible without becoming retro kitsch?
- Is the text readable on a projector?
- Are images relevant rather than decorative?
- Are data relationships clear?
- Does the slide feel intentionally designed rather than AI-generated?

If not, revise.
